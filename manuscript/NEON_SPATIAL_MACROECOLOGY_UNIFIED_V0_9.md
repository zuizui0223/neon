## 1. Ecological question: persistence across levels

A community can undergo two different changes: *which animals are detected at a station* and *how those animals distribute observed space use within and among individuals*. Spatial niche partitioning, seasonal stability and density-associated home-range changes are already well known. The question here is not simply whether a given species has a preferred habitat or whether crowding compresses a home range; it is whether recognizable taxon-associated spatial organization endures despite reconfiguration of the constituent sampled population.

We distinguish three nested observational levels:
1. **Observed marked-individual level:** repeated locations of a tag, short-term movement and W (variance of observed positions *within* individuals).
2. **Local population level:** spatial arrangement of observed individual centres (B), relative abundance or genus MNKA, and shifts in their allocation.
3. **Community and landscape level:** season-to-season recurrence of genus-associated station use, independently weighted across physical sites and ultimately compared along environmental gradients.

The hypothesis of **persistence at the community level despite plasticity below it** is biologically interesting because stable ecological organization need not imply the same stationary animals or one universally conserved density response. However, mere juxtaposition of two separate analyses does not demonstrate this cross-level decoupling; matched cohorts and an explicit contrast are required.

### Alternative explanatory processes

- **Persistent habitat/resource template:** repeated genus capture locations can reflect recurring vegetation, shelter, soil/moisture, food or predator exposure.
- **Lineage-dependent capture/detection template:** traps and behaviours can give repeated genus labels even without stable true-use niches. This is not controlled merely by holding trap *overall genus richness* constant.
- **Biotic interactions and crowding:** animal interactions can alter the distribution of individuals within a lineage and where lineages are recorded. The historical fixed-N/U conspecific/congeneric comparison has zero conservative same-season matched substitution pairs, and therefore cannot currently identify the proposed competition mechanism.
- **Source and temporal dependence:** repeated season keys, sampling windows, uneven target taxa and field identification qualifiers can all influence apparent spatial structure.

## 3. Methods: two responses, one hierarchy

### 3.1 Community-scale taxon–station recurrence

Use the **already frozen** 58 comparisons across 14 sites. For each pair, season A and B genus×station binary matrices are built after tags present in both seasons have been excluded from BOTH matrices. Overlap is `O = sum_{g,t} A[g,t]B[g,t]`. A corrected fixed-margin Markov sampler randomizes season B while preserving each genus's occupied station total and the number of genera at each station. Contrast `R_u = (O_u-E_0 O_u)/SD_0 O_u` is averaged within each physical site and then with equal site weights. The full 14-site group and prespecified-by-source 12-site exclusion sensitivity are distinct views of the **same ecological outcome**, not independent datasets.

The 2026-10-20 null correction replaced an invalid accepted-only-jump-chain clock with a symmetric proposal-clock that retains failed swaps and holding probabilities; two chains of 2,000 retained draws each were run for every unit. Finite-state target and mixing diagnostics passed. Post-result independent-unit and comonotone rank-coupled scenarios probe, but cannot fully identify, intra-site temporal dependence.


### 3.1b Post-result ecological broadening to exactly two eligible genera

The original pre-effect ≥3-genera support design had already documented **216** adjacent-season comparisons containing ≥2 supported genera, but the original effect study opened only the **58** with ≥3. The **158** exactly-two comparisons are disjoint at the site×plot×season-pair key level from the old 58 and were frozen as an *exploratory historical same-release extension* before their new spatial effect was opened. All original source, genus-level field taxonomy, nightly trap-effort, ≥2 season-exclusive individuals and ≥2 occupied stations per genus in both seasons, and exact shared-tag removal from both matrices remain identical to the old design. Source-only geographical support passed prospectively under the *new* 12-site, 8-domain, 80-pair feasibility threshold: **158 pairs, 30 sites, 15 domains**.

The null for **exactly two genus rows** has an explicit uniform finite distribution. Among season-B stations, columns of genus richness 0 or 2 are fixed; suppose `m` stations have richness exactly 1, with `a` occupied by genus1. All `C(m,a)` equally weighted assignments preserve B genus occupancy and station richness. Holding season A fixed, assign every singleton station weight `A1(t)−A2(t)∈{−1,0,1}` and enumerate the exact distribution of selected weights using the multivariate hypergeometric counts `C(n+,x) C(n−,y) C(n0,a−x−y)/C(m,a)`. This yields the exact overlap mean and variance for each seasonal pair, and Z is standardized by that **exact conditional fixed-margin null**. Brute-force synthetic full-state comparisons and row-label symmetry tests passed before source outcomes. It does not use the historically defective accepted-only-swap chain.

Before calculating any new observed spatial overlap, we checked all exact-null marginal variances. The fixed source eligibility gate required ≥75% informative among all 158 units, ≥40 informative pairs, and ≥8 independent sites. Exactly **150/158** units at **30 sites** passed (8 zero-variance units were retained in denominators but not assigned Z). We then averaged informative pair Z values within each site and sites equally; no genus, year or environmental subgroup was selected by sign. We sampled **20,000** site-equal joint statistics independently from the exact per-pair null marginals, and separately under a strong within-site shared-rank (comonotone) coupling. These are conditional null *scenarios*, not the empirical joint capture mechanism or worst-case bounds over all possible site dependencies. A source-defined **whole-physical-site** exclusion removed every site with any reused site×plot×season key, leaving **17 sites/48 informative pairs**. The exact-two site effects are **not pooled** with earlier 3+-genus site effects or San Jacinto.

### 3.1d A distinct 10 m ecological scale beyond the exact same trap

The official [NEON small mammal sampling protocol](https://www.neonscience.org/data-collection/small-mammals) fixes a physical 10×10 trapping lattice with traps 10 m apart. To ask whether observed genus-associated seasonal recurrence is broader than fixed trap identity, a new **single-radius ecological test** was committed *after* the original positive exact-trap result but **before opening any new neighboring-trap outcome**. The primary neighbor definition is the immediate four orthogonal rook-neighbors at 10 m; the self trap is excluded and no diagonal, 20m, 30m or further radius was searched. We used all same **158** source-approved original exact-two-genera comparisons, limited both seasons to the independently verified common six-night recorded station set. We preserved the original season-exclusive marked-tag rules and genus field taxonomy.

For each genus g and B-season station t, define `K_g(t)=1` if an **A-season genus-g station distinct from t** occurs at any rook-neighbor of t; the observed 10m patch score is `S=Σ_g Σ_{t∈B_g}K_g(t)`. To separate this from the already positive exact-station phenomenon, the new null additionally conditions on the *observed original same-genus same-trap overlap O*, not just fixed B genus occupancy and per-station genus richness. In two genus rows, condition on both the number `a` of B-singleton stations assigned to genus 1 and the weighted sum `Σ_t[A_1(t)−A_2(t)]X_t=O−O_{fixed}`. Under this condition, all uniform fixed-margin assignments are equally probable. We obtained the exact null score mean and variance **analytically** by summing combinatorially weighted multivariate hypergeometric moments across the three possible overlap-weight groups (−1, 0, +1). Direct IID samples of the exact conditional target were drawn for the site-level stochastic stress calibration, with no accepted-only MCMC chain. Exhaustive small-source-state enumeration tests established uniformity, invariance to genus label exchange, preservation of O and true zero-variance handling.

**Before opening the newly defined S outcome**, ≥80 of the unchanged 158 source units and ≥18 independent physical sites were required to have nonzero exact **conditional** patch-score variance; the fact that O itself was previously observed is disclosed, so this does not constitute a new independent pre-outcome hypothesis of the whole study. Exactly **153/158** original source comparisons at all **30** physical sites passed; five yielded zero conditional patch variance. The 153 standardized `Z_patch` values were averaged within physical sites and between sites with equal weights. Under fixed seed, 2,000 **IID conditional target draws per unit** and two 20,000-draw site-weighted null stress models (independent unit marginals vs perfectly rank-comonotone within physical site) were performed. A positive directional claim required positive grand Z **and** one-sided p<0.05 under both scenarios, without taxon, region or interval fishing.

The **newly fixed whole-site source reuse sensitivity** omitted *every physical site* with any repeated site×plot×meteorological-season source key in the original 158-pair cohort, regardless of its S sign; the retained subset was tested only when ≥8 independent physical sites and ≥25 informative source pairs remained. The 17 such retained sites are **not necessarily the same 17 physically new sites** compared with the earlier ≥3-genera strict cohort. We do not pool patch-score Z or p with old exact-station Z or old ≥3-genera effects; these are different conditional estimands.

### 3.6 Operationally documented trap-night source control: a pre-effect eligibility test

A further source-only observational control evaluated whether the same station was not only *listed* on all six original nights but showed evidence that its trap was actually set and checked. NEON's official `trapStatus` categorical codes explicitly distinguish **4 = more than one capture**, **5 = capture**, **6 = trap set and empty** from **0 = no data**, **1 = not set or captures not processed**, **2 = disturbed/door closed empty**, and **3 = door open with spoor** (see NEON [small mammal user guide](https://data.neonscience.org/api/v0/documents/NEON_smammal_userGuide_vF?inline=true) and [2026 official data tutorial](https://www.neonscience.org/resources/learning-hub/tutorials/mammal-data-intro)). A source row's *existence* does not indicate a fully functional trap.

The original **158** exact-two season-pair cohort was reconstructed without inspecting a new ecological effect. For every canonical station×night in the two source three-night bouts, status was parsed from the source categorical code; **only statuses 4/5/6 counted** as a documented operational encounter, and any 0/1/2/3, missing, unparseable, semantically contradictory or duplicate-row conflict disqualified that station-night. The intersection across all six source nights yielded the strict six-night set `E6`. Both original genus-by-station matrices would be restricted to `E6`, preserving original nonshared animal-tag filtering and requiring ≥2 captured stations per genus in each season. **Before any new seasonal genus×trap overlap could be opened**, the newly frozen support gate required |E6|≥90 for at least **80** season pairs across **18** physical sites and **10** NEON ecological domains. Failure produces a recorded data-support STOP; it cannot be rescued by reducing the 90-position cutoff or including disturbed/unset traps.

Importantly, codes 4/5 are *capture outcomes*, while code 6 is a trap reported set but empty. Conditioning on the all-six-night intersection is therefore a conservative **coverage/sampling selection diagnostic**, not an independent causal detection-probability correction: source animal capture can affect status itself, statuses 2/3 may indicate traps that were usable before disturbance, and genus-dependent catchability remains unobserved. Source-domain and source-year eligibility are retained in the aggregate record; source trap or individual identities are never exported.


### 3.7 A single genus comparison of observed within-bout re-encounter propensity

A source-only genus-specific observation-process contrast was frozen **after** the established genus-at-exact-trap recurrence and the failed strict all-six-night trap-operability source gate, **before** any subsequent-day tag re-encounter rates were opened. This is not a relaxation of that old operability gate: the new estimand is `P(observed captured again on day 2 or 3 of the SAME three-night bout | tagged and captured on day 1)`, with unspecified availability and trap functioning. NEON [protocol](https://www.neonscience.org/data-collection/small-mammals) and [official 2026 tutorial](https://www.neonscience.org/resources/learning-hub/tutorials/mammal-data-intro) document night-UID, capture/status and marking of individual small mammals. The existing exported basic source parser does not carry capture fate, so a first-night animal is **not assumed known released alive and available** for two subsequent nights.

From the unchanged **158** exact-two-genus source seasonal pairs at 30 sites, we reconstructed **293 distinct original three-night bouts** (the same bout can appear in adjacent seasonal pairs). The actual first night was identified by its original verified capture date, never nightuid lexicographic sort. A valid first-night cohort member required a marked tag, official capture-status 4/5, valid canonical source station, consistent target genus from authoritative NEON taxonomy and no conflicting original genus assignment across same site×plot bouts or ambiguous multiple first-night stations. These membership checks used **only first-night marked captures** plus whole-source genus identity validation. In this source stage there were **3,255 eligible first-night tag×bout records at 30 physical sites spanning 15 genera**; no later-night re-encounter indicator was computed.

A prospective *within-historical-study* source gate was committed before the new outcome: ≥400 tagged first-night observations at ≥15 physical sites overall, two source-selected genera with ≥100 first-night tagged animals at ≥6 physical sites each, and ≥6 **shared** physical sites each having ≥5 tagged first-night observations for both genera. All candidate genus pairs were ranked **only by first-night sample denominators** (maximum smaller sample, then total count, then taxon name for ties). Thus **no retroactive relabeling to `RELEASED_EXPLICIT` is authorized**, and the previous 7-site outcome/thresholds remain unchanged.

The source stage is inspectable in `results/neon_firstnight_original_fate_source_20261028.json`, supported by [Actions 40535085090](https://github.com/zuizui0223/neon/actions/runs/40535085090), and code `analysis/neon_macro_firstnight_fate_source_v1.py`. NEON documents the separate `categoricalCodes_10072` source file for controlled-value semantics [tutorial](https://www.neonscience.org/resources/learning-hub/tutorials/mammal-data-intro).

### 4.7. Historical canopy images actually exist at capture-dated mammal stations, but mammal habitat selection remains untested

For the frozen original 58 seasonal comparisons, the published nearest-acquisition metadata upper bound was 36/58 pairs within 12 calendar months and 22/58 within three months. The actual AOP source check recovered all **31/31** selected published monthly CHM manifests and **28/28** months needed within the ±12-month original source group. **51** original 1-km GeoTIFFs were downloaded and provider size/checksum validated, yielding **24,600/24,600** valid original plot×capture-day×station 1 m canopy pixels at **246 unique plot×date keys**. Zero-coverage and masked records were not replaced by imputation; none occurred within the prespecified candidate. Exactly **36/58** original seasonal pairs with all six trapping dates had >=95 verified canopy pixels on every date, covering **12 independent physical sites / 21 grids**. Thus the descriptive historical environment source-coverage criterion passed (20 pairs/8 sites/12 plots minimum). Among stricter prior/same calendar-month matched images, **18/58** pairs (six sites/10 grids) qualified, insufficient for the same eight-site minimum; 10/58 original pairs in six sites had prior/same acquisition months within three months. Both positive and limited source counts are preserved.

This result improves **environmental data availability**, not an effect estimate. AOP pixels may precede or follow captures by up to 12 calendar months, one coordinate has a geoNEON assumption-based median uncertainty ~3.1 m relative to 1 m image pixels, local uncertainty windows varied in CHM height in 8,637 station×capture-day evaluations, and 300 window samples were clipped at a tile edge. Moreover, the NEON CHM production threshold of 2 m cannot describe much of the shrub and grassy habitat at the rodent trap. No genus×station canopy association, season-to-season habitat stability parameter, trap catchability correction, ecological competition model or R–W/B correlation was calculated in this source audit. The original station recurrence is still an observation-only ecological association.

### 4.8 Independent plant and ground-cover field measurements at nearby base plots: source GO, not true trap microhabitat

An independent original NEON RELEASE-2026 Plant presence and percent cover (DP1.10058.001) source audit retrieved and verified **326** original 1m2 plant survey CSVs (355,486 total dated rows). Every one of the original **30 mammal grid plotIDs** was associated with at least one matching base-plot field-plant survey record from the same source site; 27,854 plant records shared one of those plot IDs, with 27,235 rows containing numeric plant or ground-cover data. This is a sample-metadata source result; no vegetation cover magnitude or mammal habitat effect was computed.

The 58 original mammal paired seasons were assessed against independent plant survey event dates using frozen 90-day and 365-day windows. Counting only TWO DISTINCT survey events with at least one numerical vegetation-cover observation linked to the original seasons, **51/58 original season pairs (14 independent sites/28 plots)** were covered within 365 days, while **16/58 (seven sites/11 plots)** were covered within 90 days. The source-only minimum 20 pairs/eight sites/12 plots passes at 365 days but fails at 90 days. Combined with 36/58 original pairs having independently measured historical canopy-source coverage within 12 months, the set-overlap bounds on joint environmental *availability* are **29 to 36/58 pairs** under the lenient field-plant window (exact overlap not directly cross-tabulated); no strict 90-day combined-source support is claimed.

NEON distributed mammal grids are designed to be offset roughly **150±50 m** from the matched Distributed base plant plot despite the shared plotID. Therefore numeric plant/herb/ground-cover data are a landscape-neighborhood context measure, NOT independently measured habitat at each of the original 100 live trap stations. The long 365-day window does not establish within-season plant cover or vegetation stability. Independent genus-by-trap catchability and animal occupancy/use remain unobserved and the old original corrected seasonal capture-location effects are not refitted here.

## 5. Discussion and proposed generality

**New genus-specific re-encounter heterogeneity qualifies the observation process.** Microtus and Peromyscus differ in the first-night-marked cohort's subsequent two-night recapture fraction within seven *source-selected matched physical sites* (−23.5 percentage points, whole-site 95% interval −45.7 to −2.4). Because this quantity conflates re-encounter chance with survival/release, movement, behavior and availability, it does **not** estimate a genus-specific trap-level detection probability. The result nevertheless makes a universal, equal capture/recapture process across all genera a questionable *unverified assumption*. The central exact-station recurrence result is still a genuine **conditional capture-space pattern**, but ecological habitat memory/lineage preferences remain unproven. The original `fate` source audit adds another concrete limitation: new genus-specific trap-detection claims require distinguishing first-night disposition and later reencounter; our conservative explicit-release source subset was too thin and post-2017 fate categories need source-dictionary verification. A −23.5 point same-bout return difference is not a causal demonstration of unequal capture probability. A future causal model needs an independently observed movement/occupancy stream or verified trap-by-night functioning and release histories, not another post-result threshold adjustment.

The pattern established today is **organizational recurrence beyond repeated observation of the same individuals at fixed named capture stations, under source-conditional observation assumptions**. A newly checked NEON controlled-status source audit directly constrains that inference: all 158 earlier two-genus pairs had nearly complete *recorded* station rosters, but only 18 supported a stringent six-night operational-status intersection with ≥90 stations. No new operability-filtered ecological recurrence score was computed because source support was too sparse. Therefore we cannot now assert that even the exact-station signal has been independently corrected for trap function or genus-specific capture propensity. The new conditional 10 m patch test places an important *spatial-scale limit* on its ecological interpretation: full-data conditional neighbor enrichment is modest, but the positive support **does not persist when all source-season reused sites are excluded**. We must therefore distinguish the comparatively robust taxon-associated exact-station capture signature from an **unproven broader patch reassembly mechanism**. A uniform fixed-margin null cannot distinguish spatially correlated capture propensity from stable actual animal-use preference at neighboring stations, even after conditioning on exact trap identity. Matching the documented station set across all six nights provides an additional bounded observation-process control: the positive site-level sign survives with minimal numerical attenuation. The separate single-axis tower-temperature hypothesis remains **UNTESTED**, because the frozen site-month weather-QC support criterion failed by one original pair, not because of an ecological null.


**New empirically tested macro generality (bounded).** The original 14-site taxon signature recurrence is not an isolated consequence of requiring three or more sampled genera: the structurally separate two-genus seasonal comparisons yielded concordant positive average site effects at 30 physical sites and covered five additional ecological domains relative to the original ≥3 genus cohort. Among the 17 source-defined physical sites absent from the original ≥3-genus cohort, 15 had positive mean recurrence (Z=+0.760). This is stronger evidence of a geographically wider observational pattern than simply including the 13 overlapping sites. Nonetheless the source-release cohort is historical, the within-site rank-dependent null is only a sensitivity scenario, and the additional whole-site reused-season exclusion is underpowered; hence this is **geographic nonoverlap robustness and within-release exploratory consistency, not independent prospective replication**. The two-row exact null also eliminates a specific computational uncertainty at that taxonomic richness; it cannot eliminate systematic genus-dependent trap propensity or persistent habitat fields.

 The historical density component does not establish a complementary conserved law: it instead warns that density-associated within-vs-among individual geometry varies among lineages and has observational limitations. This contrast motivates a stronger and genuinely macroecological next question: *Is high-level spatial organization buffered against lower-level population rearrangement, and do environmental heterogeneity and temporal constancy predict the limits of this buffering?*


**Novelty boundary against recent NEON macroecology.** Holian et al. (2026, *Ecology and Evolution*, DOI [10.1002/ece3.73214](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.73214)) already examined continental NEON small-mammal **spatial and temporal occupancy–abundance relationships**, including 127 species/40 genera. Brownfield et al. (2025, *Oecologia*, DOI [10.1007/s00442-025-05731-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-025-05731-2)) analysed density, latitude and habitat effects on deer mouse space use across 36 NEON sites, and Barton et al. (2026, *Ecology and Evolution*, DOI [10.1002/ece3.73413](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.73413)) documented North American rodent population synchrony. Our unique current observable is specifically **the same genus recurring at the same trap station between adjacent seasons after excluding all shared observed tag identities, beyond fixed genus/station margins**. This differs from simply recording occupancy through time. Its predictive climate/biome generality remains untested, especially in light of the source eligibility bottleneck.

The key prediction is cross-level, not a generic rediscovery of rodent niche segregation. A valuable **negative** future result would be that apparently persistent taxon signatures disappear when genus-specific detection is measured independently; another would be that few source populations support both nested responses, making direct coupling empirically unidentifiable in the current NEON data. Neither should be hidden by exploratory subgroup selection.

Prior literature establishes separate axes of habitat association (Goldingay & Price 1997; Chock et al. 2022), density and space-use variation (e.g. O'Fallon et al., *Oecologia*, 2025), and continental population synchrony (Barton et al., *Ecology and Evolution*, 2026). Continental coverage of NEON by itself is not novelty. What could be novel is **a genuinely matched ecological test of the stability of taxon-to-place structure versus the plasticity of individual/population space use, with site-level environmental and detection boundaries**.

## 7. Conclusion

**Established under specified conditional observational nulls at exact trap locations (not demonstrated as general 10m patch persistence):** Genus-associated capture locations can recur across adjacent seasons without requiring observation of the same tagged individuals in both seasons. The original corrected 3+-genus 14-site pattern is complemented by a **new exact-two-genus, post-result exploratory 30-site pattern** that supports the same direction in less genus-rich observed communities without pooling the two effects. **Separately observed:** density response heterogeneity with a failed original generality criterion. **New structural finding:** only 12/58 original seasonal comparisons had same-genus repeat-supported opportunities for direct cross-scale source linking; direct joint ecological inference did not meet its newly fixed sample-support rule. **Separately observed:** Microtus and Peromyscus differ in their source-matched subsequent-night *re-encounter fractions*, not proven trap-level detection probabilities or a causal source of between-season recurrence. **Not established:** universal density accommodation, a causal or predictive cross-level linkage, a climatic/macroscale mechanism, competition, or habitat-driven recurrence. We proceed as **one** falsifiable macroecological programme, not two successfully validated papers.
