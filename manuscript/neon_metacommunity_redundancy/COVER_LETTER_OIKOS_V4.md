# Cover letter — Oikos, V4

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Paper, **“Small-mammal spatial continuity is locally redundant but carried by different species across sites,”** for publication in *Oikos*.

Ecological aggregation is often interpreted through complementarity: different species compensate for one another and generate an aggregate property that no component provides alone. We tested whether that logic extends to the spatial continuity of observed occurrences in small-mammal metacommunities.

Before opening biological responses, we froze 16 fresh sites from the National Ecological Observatory Network, geolocatable trap nodes, prespecified trap-neighbourhood adjacency criteria, target taxa, and a community-minus-best-species continuity contrast. All sites completed the once-only programme. Pooling species increased continuity at 0 of 16 sites; the median gain was zero and the preregistered null-adjusted sign test gave p = 1.0. At every site, at least one individual species satisfied all prespecified criteria, and no strict emergent criterion occurred in which the pooled community was continuous while all individual species failed.

The result is not simply an absence of turnover. A post hoc audit restricted to frozen site summaries revealed a second scale of organization: 48 site-level continuity-carrier records were distributed across 32 species, 20 carrier species occurred at only one site, no species occurred as a carrier at more than three sites, and only 18 of 120 site pairs shared any carrier species. Thus the aggregate property was **redundant within sites but carried by different taxa among sites**.

Richer sites contained more individually sufficient species while single-species dominance declined, yet the fraction of eligible species that were sufficient did not increase. At one site, taxonomic pooling also reduced the continuity fraction from 1.0 for the best species to 0.25 for the pooled community under the declared all-positive definition, illustrating a weakest-link outcome.

We use “redundancy” only in a property-specific sense: several species independently satisfy the same declared spatial-continuity criterion. We do not imply general ecological interchangeability or functional equivalence, and we do not interpret the post hoc carrier-turnover pattern as temporal insurance.

The manuscript does not cite prior work by the author and is not a taxon-specific extension of an author-developed empirical result. Instead, it builds on published metacommunity, spatial-insurance and redundancy concepts by testing their complementarity logic against a distinct aggregate spatial property. We believe this general distinction among complementarity, property-specific redundancy and carrier turnover is well aligned with the broad ecological scope of *Oikos*.

The confirmatory endpoint is closed and auditable. No sites, thresholds, taxa or confirmatory decision rules were retuned after biological-response access. An anonymous data-and-code review archive accompanies the submission.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
