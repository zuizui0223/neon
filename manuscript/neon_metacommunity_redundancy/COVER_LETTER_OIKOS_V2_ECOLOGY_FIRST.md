# Cover letter — Oikos

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Community spatial continuity is species-redundant rather than turnover-generated in small-mammal metacommunities,”** for publication in *Oikos*.

Ecological aggregation is often interpreted through complementarity: heterogeneous species or local communities may compensate for one another and generate an aggregate property that no component provides alone. Our study asks whether that logic extends to a spatial property of metacommunities. Specifically, we tested prospectively whether pooling small-mammal species creates spatial continuity that is absent from every individual species.

We used 16 fresh NSF NEON small-mammal sites selected without biological-response access. Before opening responses, we froze site selection, geolocatable trap nodes, prespecified trap-neighbourhood adjacency criteria, target taxa, the community-minus-best-species contrast, and the null analysis. All 16 sites completed the once-only programme.

The predicted complementarity did not occur. Pooling species increased continuity at 0 of 16 sites; the median gain was zero and the preregistered null-adjusted sign test gave p = 1.0. At every site, at least one individual species satisfied every prespecified adjacency criterion, and no strict emergent criterion occurred in which the community was continuous while all individual species failed. Cross-species-only local support was rare. At one site, ORNL, taxonomic pooling reduced the continuity fraction from 1.0 for the best species to 0.25 for the pooled community under the declared all-positive definition.

We interpret this as a distinction between **complementarity and redundancy in aggregate ecological properties**. Community spatial continuity in these systems was already contained within species-level occurrence structures rather than created by species turnover. The ORNL case further shows that aggregation can impose a weakest-link constraint when every added positive occurrence must itself be spatially supported.

This is not a direct falsification of spatial-insurance theory, and the manuscript does not infer dispersal mechanisms from the adjacency criteria. Instead, it tests whether a general complementarity logic familiar from insurance and compensatory frameworks transfers to observed spatial continuity. We believe this distinction speaks to broad questions in metacommunity ecology about when regional properties genuinely emerge from species turnover and when they are inherited redundantly from component species.

The confirmatory endpoint is closed and auditable. No sites, thresholds, taxa, or decision rules were retuned after biological-response access.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.

Sincerely,

ZHANG Ruiqi
