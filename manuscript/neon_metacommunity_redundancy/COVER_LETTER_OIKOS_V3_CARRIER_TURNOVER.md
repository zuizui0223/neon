# Cover letter — Oikos, V3

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Community spatial continuity is species-redundant within sites but carried by different taxa across small-mammal metacommunities,”** for publication in *Oikos*.

Ecological aggregation can produce emergent properties through complementarity, but an aggregate property may also be redundant when several component species can provide it independently. We tested this distinction prospectively for the spatial continuity of observed small-mammal occurrences.

Before opening biological responses, we froze 16 fresh NSF NEON sites, geolocatable trap nodes, prespecified trap-neighbourhood adjacency criteria, target taxa, and a community-minus-best-species continuity contrast. All 16 sites completed the once-only programme. Pooling species increased continuity at 0 of 16 sites; the median gain was zero and the preregistered null-adjusted sign test gave p = 1.0. At every site, at least one individual species satisfied all prespecified criteria, and no strict emergent criterion occurred in which the pooled community was continuous while all individual species failed.

The result is not simply an absence of turnover. A post hoc audit restricted to the frozen site summaries revealed a second, positive scale of organization. The 48 site-level continuity-carrier records were distributed across 32 species; 20 carrier species occurred at only one site, no species at more than three sites, and only 18 of 120 site pairs shared any carrier species. Thus continuity was **redundant within sites but taxonomically replaceable among sites**.

Richer sites contained more individually sufficient species while single-species dominance declined, yet the fraction of eligible species that were sufficient did not increase. This distinguishes deepening redundancy from turnover-generated local rescue. At ORNL, taxonomic pooling additionally reduced the continuity fraction from 1.0 for the best species to 0.25 for the pooled community under the declared all-positive definition, illustrating a weakest-link outcome.

We use “redundancy” only in this property-specific sense: several species independently satisfy the same declared spatial-continuity criterion. We do not imply general ecological interchangeability or functional equivalence, and we do not interpret the post hoc carrier-turnover pattern as temporal insurance.

We believe the study offers a general ecological distinction between **complementarity, redundancy, and carrier turnover** in aggregate community properties, supported by a response-naive confirmatory design and a clearly separated exploratory extension.

Sincerely,

ZHANG Ruiqi
