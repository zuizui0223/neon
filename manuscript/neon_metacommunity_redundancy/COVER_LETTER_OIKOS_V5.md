# Cover letter — Oikos, V5

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Paper, **“Local spatial cohesion in small mammals is redundant across species but context-dependent across sites,”** for publication in *Oikos*.

Ecological aggregation is often interpreted through complementarity: different species compensate for one another and generate an aggregate property that no component provides alone. We tested whether that logic extends to a stringent occurrence-geometry property in small-mammal metacommunities: local spatial cohesion, defined by the absence of isolated positive traps under prespecified neighbourhood criteria.

The study contains two explicitly separated response-naive components. First, before opening biological responses, we froze 16 National Ecological Observatory Network sites, trap nodes, target taxa, adjacency criteria and a pooled-community-minus-best-species contrast. Pooling species increased cohesion at 0 of 16 sites; the median gain was zero and the preregistered null-adjusted sign test gave p = 1.0. Every site contained at least one individual species satisfying all declared criteria, and no strict emergent criterion occurred.

The result was nevertheless ecologically structured. A frozen post hoc audit found 48 site-level carrier records distributed among 32 species, with median pairwise carrier-set Jaccard similarity of zero. The carrier set spanned 13 granivores, 10 herbivores, seven omnivores and two predators. Among 13 original sites with multiple carriers, 12 contained carriers from more than one primary trophic group, and two sites contained granivorous, herbivorous, omnivorous and predatory carriers simultaneously. Thus redundancy in the spatial property did not imply redundancy in ecosystem-effect roles.

Second, after the original endpoint was closed, we prospectively froze an independent mechanism programme on all 11 remaining structurally eligible sites before opening their biological responses. Conditioning on each species' observed number of positive traps did not reveal positive spatial organization beyond prevalence: median site carrier excess was -0.0804, only 4 of 11 sites were positive, and the one-sided exact sign test gave p = 0.8867. A predeclared grid-conditioned null then showed where the remaining spatial structure resided. Preserving each species' positive-trap count within mammal grids reproduced the observed carrier state almost exactly (66 of 68 eligible species × site records), leaving a median within-grid component of zero. Among species occurring at multiple fresh sites, 10 of 15 switched between carrier and non-carrier states.

These results sharpen the ecological interpretation. A carrier is not a universal taxon, trophic guild, mobility syndrome or generalist type. It is a context-dependent species × site spatial state. Ecologically different species can converge on the same local cohesion property, while the same species can leave that state when its local occurrence allocation changes. We therefore distinguish this metric explicitly from whole-landscape dispersal connectivity and from functional redundancy in ecosystem functioning.

The manuscript does not cite prior work by the author and is not a taxon-specific extension of an author-developed empirical result. It builds on metacommunity, spatial-insurance, redundancy and response–effect concepts to distinguish complementarity, property-specific redundancy, taxonomic replaceability and context-dependent spatial response states.

Both response programmes are closed and auditable. No sites, thresholds, taxa or primary decision rules were retuned after biological-response access. An anonymous data-and-code review archive containing the frozen protocols, derived results, tests and figure-generation code accompanies the submission.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
