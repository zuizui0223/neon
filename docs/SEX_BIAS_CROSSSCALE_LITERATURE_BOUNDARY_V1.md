# Literature boundary — sex-bias cross-scale transfer

Date: 2026-09-29

## Not novel

Scaling individual movement processes to population-level spatial patterns is an established theoretical and methodological problem.

Examples include:
- Potts, Mokross & Lewis (2014/2015): frameworks deriving population-level spatial patterns from individual movement, resource selection and interaction processes.
- Dupont et al. (2022): integration of movement models with spatial capture-recapture to improve population-level connectivity inference.
- Buderman et al. (2025), Methods in Ecology and Evolution 16:345–361: Integrated Movement Models formally combine individual tracking and species-distribution data to estimate population-scale movement processes.
- recent range-resident population models explicitly connect individual home-range movement to population-level spatial consequences.

Therefore this programme must not claim novelty for the idea that individual movement scales up to population distributions.

## Distinct empirical test

The prospective contribution, if estimable, is narrower:

> Across multiple small-mammal species-site populations sampled under a common NEON protocol, does the observed male-female contrast in repeated-night movement covary with the independently observed male-female contrast in one-night population spatial packing?

Distinctive features:
- paired species×site comparison of two biological scales;
- one standardized capture system rather than combining telemetry and broad species-distribution data;
- sex contrast as the shared signed perturbation across scales;
- exact conditioning of packing on captured N and realized trap geometry if the effect stage is authorized;
- previously inspected sex-effect species removed before the feasibility scan.

## Claim boundary

Even a positive transfer result would not establish that movement alone causes packing. Social interactions, habitat selection, detectability, turnover and sampling scale can mediate or break the mapping.

The strongest allowed interpretation would be empirical cross-scale concordance of sex bias, not a general mechanistic derivation from movement to population distribution.
