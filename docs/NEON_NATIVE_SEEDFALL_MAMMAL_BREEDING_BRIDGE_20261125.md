## Prior art points to the animal, not just the plant

Stephens et al. (2024), J Animal Ecology, DOI 10.1111/1365-2656.14148, 12 years at Bartlett, six mast events, five species, Results Fig 6 and discussion (https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2656.14148). Reported lactation advances in years of early rather than late seed availability: P. leucopus 24 d, N. insignis 37 d, B. brevicauda 51 d, P. maniculatus 79 d; M. gapperi exceptional second breeding period. Even the two congeneric Peromyscus differ in reported mean offset by 55 d (arithmetic from original reported values, not a new test). Strong species-level caveat for genus-level inference. The paper explicitly reports concurrent and following-year reproductive pulses and diet/overwintering differences. Do not claim resource timing, cache-mediated capital breeding, or these species differences as our novelty. McLean & Guralnick 2021 already use NEON and museum breeding records for broad-scale climate/reproductive phenology. The possible new question is whether within-species **interannual phase shifts track measured resource inputs across independent sites** and whether their predictive support exceeds calendar, weather and previous-year population controls. No claim that source paper established NEON mechanisms.
