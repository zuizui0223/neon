# Temporal-aliasing literature boundary v1

Date: 2026-09-29

## Not novel

Temporal aggregation of capture/detection data is already known to discard information.

- Borchers et al. (2014), Methods in Ecology and Evolution, continuous-time spatially explicit capture-recapture: exact detection times are often aggregated into occasions; this can discard information and makes occasion definition subjective.
- Efford (2019), Methods in Ecology and Evolution: detector models differ in whether multiple detections within an occasion are possible and whether they are represented as binary or count data.
- Continuous-time and recent movement-aware SCR developments explicitly model detection timing or dependence rather than assuming all within-occasion detections are interchangeable.

Therefore this programme must not claim novelty for the general statement that temporal aggregation loses information.

## Distinct empirical question here

The San Jacinto live-trapping protocol provides repeated handling/release and re-capture opportunities within the same night, with exact capture time and 6.25 m trap-grid position.

The held-out question is:

> How large is the spatial aliasing introduced when repeated within-night captures of a small mammal are collapsed to one nightly location, and does that aliasing propagate more strongly into individual movement summaries than into population spatial-configuration summaries?

Potentially distinct contributions, only if the held-out Cricetidae validation passes its frozen gates:

- empirical magnitude of first-to-last within-night location instability in live-trapped small mammals;
- cross-species replication outside the discovery heteromyids;
- direct comparison of representative-location sensitivity at individual-movement versus population-configuration scales;
- a reproducible diagnostic for whether one-position-per-night reduction is defensible for a given live-trapping protocol.

## Claim boundary

Even with a positive validation, do not claim:

- discovery of continuous-time capture-recapture;
- that all capture occasions should always be continuous-time models;
- that trap-based home-range estimates are generally invalid;
- biological movement differences from observation-process aliasing alone.

The strongest allowed interpretation would be that temporal aggregation can be quantitatively consequential in a repeated-check live-trapping protocol and that the consequence depends on the ecological quantity being estimated.
