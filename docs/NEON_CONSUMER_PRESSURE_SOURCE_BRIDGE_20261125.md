## Li & Zhang 2007 published within-site contrast

DOI 10.1016/j.foreco.2007.01.063, Table 1 and sections 3.1, 3.3, 3.5. Full-text PDF extracted; screenshot requests timed out, so no claim of visual verification. Distinct 2000/2001 release cohorts but same Donglingshan forest cluster as Lu & Zhang 2004. 20 stations × 40 seeds × 3 seasons × 2 years. Seed observation 2 months except May 2000 (3 months); marked cache sites rechecked next May. Trap success 3% in 2000, 13.5% in 2001. Published 344 versus 89 single-seed caches, and three confirmed germinated seeds in each annual cohort. Observed cache risk 14.33% vs 3.71%, ratio 3.865; recorded germination rate 0.125% in each. Ratio of recorded germinations per recorded cache 0.259 (0.87% vs 3.37%); a descriptive ratio of published counts, not an independently estimated probability of true survival. Unknown/unlocated fates, follow-up differences and six germinations preclude causal or equivalence claim. The published seasonal cache peak changes from August in 2000 to May in 2001. Resource supply and rodent abundance co-vary, two years only; no attribution of the change to either separately. No raw seed counts inferred from percentages, no new significance test.

## New external original sources

Primary: Zwolak et al. 2024, Journal of Ecology 112:1940–1951, DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.14356, author-linked public OSF DOI 10.17605/osf.io/56m2n. Six 1-ha plots, 90 capture site/session/year combinations, 7,054 offered acorns and 2,999 removed (removal includes consumption at station). Seed supply experimentally standardized while mouse abundance varies. Original paper already reports increasing then saturating removal, decreasing recovery, lower caching and greater consumption at high mouse abundance; burial emergence benefits depend on abiotic stress. **This is prior art, not a new NEON result.** Separate experiments on seedling emergence cannot be assumed to trace the same released seeds. Study periods June–September; not an autumn-versus-spring contrast. Need exact provider schema before a new contrast. Potential distinct question: how much of density-associated variation in recorded seed fate is accounted for by encounter/removal versus post-harvest fate, and is the relationship stable across held-out site-year contexts? Use original release denominators and missing fates; avoid multiplying unlinked marginal outcomes. First source inventory only; no new model or ecological effect.
