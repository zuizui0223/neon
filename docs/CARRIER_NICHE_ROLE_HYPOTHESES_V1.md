# Continuity carriers: niche and ecosystem-role hypotheses

## Current result

The carrier-turnover result becomes more informative when carrier identity is compared with ecological niche.

The literature-backed audit now codes all 32 carrier species. The earlier 17-species pilot was expanded before interpreting trait frequencies.

The 32 carriers span four coarse primary trophic groups:

- **13 granivores**;
- **10 herbivores**;
- **7 omnivores**;
- **2 predators**.

All four species that recur as carriers at three sites span three primary trophic groups: omnivory, granivory and herbivory.

More importantly, 13 of the 16 sites contain at least two independent continuity carriers. **Twelve of those 13 multi-carrier sites contain carriers from more than one primary trophic group.** NOGP is the sole within-guild case, with two herbivorous `Microtus` species.

MOAB and OAES are stronger still: each contains independent carriers from **all four** coarse trophic groups — granivore, herbivore, omnivore and predator.

### Strong within-site examples

**JORN — Chihuahuan Desert**

The carrier set includes seed-eating `Dipodomys ordii` and `Perognathus flavus`, but also `Onychomys arenicola`, whose diet is dominated by animal prey. The same spatial-continuity property is therefore independently achieved by both granivorous prey and a small-mammal/invertebrate predator.

**MOAB — Colorado Plateau**

All four carriers span the four coarse trophic roles:

- `Dipodomys ordii` — granivore;
- `Neotoma lepida` — herbivore/folivore/granivore;
- `Onychomys leucogaster` — predator/invertivore;
- `Peromyscus boylii` — omnivore.

OAES independently reproduces the same four-guild structure with `Chaetodipus hispidus`, `Neotoma floridana`, `Onychomys leucogaster`, `Reithrodontomys fulvescens` and `Sigmodon hispidus`.

These are the clearest current examples that continuity redundancy is not synonymous with trophic redundancy.

**ORNL — Appalachian forest mosaic**

`Ochrotomys nuttalli` is primarily granivorous and associated with dense woody vegetation, while `Oryzomys palustris` is semiaquatic and omnivorous, with carnivore-dominated diets reported in some marsh populations. Both are independently sufficient carriers despite strongly different niches and space-use modes.

## Conceptual correction

The term **functional redundancy** is too strong if it is read in the biodiversity–ecosystem-function sense.

A continuity carrier is defined by an observed spatial property:

> its occupied traps satisfy every prespecified adjacency criterion at a site.

That is best treated as a **site-scale spatial response property**.

It is not an ecosystem effect function such as:

- seed dispersal;
- herbivory;
- seed predation;
- invertebrate predation;
- habitat engineering;
- trophic support for predators.

The carrier species clearly differ on those effect axes.

This suggests a response–effect decoupling:

> **different ecological niches and ecosystem roles can converge on the same spatial response property.**

This distinction follows the response-trait versus effect-trait logic of Lavorel & Garnier (2002). It also prevents an unsupported jump from spatial redundancy to classical functional redundancy.

## Working causal model

The current best mechanism is hierarchical.

### 1. Regional/environmental filtering selects the candidate species pool

The species identities match their landscape contexts.

- JORN is arid grassland/desert shrubland; heteromyid granivores and `Onychomys` dominate the carrier list.
- KONA is cultivated cropland; `Sigmodon hispidus` and other grass/open-land rodents occur as carriers.
- LENO is seasonally flooded bottomland hardwood; `Peromyscus gossypinus` and `Sigmodon hispidus` are carriers.
- MOAB is a dry Colorado Plateau grass/forb–shrub landscape; `Dipodomys`, `Neotoma` and `Peromyscus` all become carriers.
- SOAP is mixed conifer forest in the Sierra Nevada; `Peromyscus boylii` is a carrier.

Thus no continent-wide carrier taxon is required.

### 2. Local spatial prevalence determines which members of that pool become carriers

A species becomes a carrier when its occurrence is sufficiently repeated across the local trap network that positive traps do not become isolated under the declared adjacency criteria.

This predicts that **within-site occupied-trap breadth** will be a much stronger predictor of carrier status than trophic guild.

The existing evidence points in this direction:

- median site-level dominance coverage is 0.835;
- 10/16 sites have one species covering at least 80% of guild-positive traps;
- cross-species-only rescue is extremely rare.

### 3. Richness adds alternative carriers rather than creating heterospecific bridges

Richer sites contain more individually sufficient species, but the fraction of eligible species that are sufficient does not rise.

That is consistent with a sampling/portfolio mechanism:

> richer local species pools contain more opportunities for one or several species to achieve broad local spatial occupancy.

It is not evidence that trophically complementary species cooperate to build continuity.

## Competing mechanism hypotheses to test

### H1 — Spatial prevalence hypothesis — current leading hypothesis

Carrier probability increases with:

- occupied-trap count;
- occupied-trap fraction;
- spatial extent of occupied traps;
- repeated detection across sampling bouts.

Prediction: these variables explain carrier status after controlling for site.

### H2 — Habitat-match hypothesis

Carrier status depends on how well a species' habitat niche matches the dominant and spatially connected habitat at a site.

Prediction: species can be narrow specialists yet become carriers where their preferred habitat is spatially extensive.

This explains why generalism alone cannot be the whole mechanism.

### H3 — Ecological-generalism hypothesis

Broad-habitat, disturbance-tolerant or omnivorous species should recur as carriers across multiple sites.

The four most recurrent carriers are compatible with this idea, but they already span three trophic guilds. The likely commonality is therefore **habitat/response breadth**, not diet.

### H4 — Mobility hypothesis

Species with larger routine movement or home-range scales may connect more trap neighborhoods.

This is plausible but currently untested. Several carriers are sedentary/fossorial, so mobility cannot be assumed to be universally high.

### H5 — Detection/abundance artifact

Abundant or highly trappable species may appear spatially continuous simply because they are detected at more traps.

This must be separated from ecological occupancy before making a causal trait claim.

Required controls:

- total capture count;
- number of trap-nights;
- occupied-trap count;
- repeated-capture frequency;
- ideally occupancy/detection modeling if the sampling structure permits it.

### H6 — Habitat heterogeneity / weakest-link mechanism

At heterogeneous sites, adding habitat-restricted species can add peripheral positive traps to the pooled community.

Prediction: pooled continuity can decline even while several component species are individually continuous.

ORNL is the current example and should be treated as a mechanism candidate, not evidence that biodiversity is harmful.

## Ecosystem-role interpretation

The carrier set spans distinct ecosystem effects.

- **seed predation / caching / potential seed dispersal:** `Dipodomys`, `Perognathus`, some `Peromyscus`;
- **green-plant consumption / herbivory:** `Microtus`, `Sigmodon`, `Synaptomys`, `Lemmiscus`;
- **invertebrate and small-vertebrate predation:** `Onychomys arenicola`;
- **mixed omnivory and trophic flexibility:** `Peromyscus boylii`, `P. gossypinus`, `Mus musculus`;
- **habitat engineering through large dens/middens:** `Neotoma`;
- **wetland/semiaquatic consumer niche:** `Oryzomys palustris`;
- **prey-base role:** nearly all of these small mammals link primary/secondary production to snakes, raptors and mammalian predators.

The striking pattern is therefore **effect-role divergence with spatial-response convergence**.

## What can be claimed now

Supported as an exploratory, literature-backed all-carrier result:

> Spatial-continuity redundancy is usually cross-trophic. Among the 13 sites with more than one carrier, 12 contain carriers from multiple primary trophic groups, and MOAB and OAES span all four coded groups. Species with very different diets, habitat niches and ecosystem effects can independently carry the same local spatial-continuity property.

Not yet supported:

> These species are functionally redundant for ecosystem functioning.

Also not yet supported:

> One trait such as generalism, body size or mobility causes carrier status.

## Next quantitative endpoint

Do not retune the closed confirmatory endpoint.

Build a separate exploratory mechanism table at the species × site level and model:

`carrier_status ~ occupied_trap_fraction + capture_rate + habitat_match + habitat_breadth + mobility + trophic_group + (1|site) + (1|species)`

The main causal contrast should be:

> **Does trophic/ecosystem-role identity add explanatory power after local spatial prevalence and detection are accounted for?**

The current prediction is **little or none**. If so, carrier identity is fundamentally a spatial response property, not a trophic functional guild.
