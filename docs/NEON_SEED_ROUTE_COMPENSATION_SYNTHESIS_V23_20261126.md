## Common definitions and orientation correction
Use A and B, successful state per originally supplied seed in source and moved routes (Sachser: in/ex-situ position only); T=A+B. Successful state is explicitly endpoint-specific: early intact/unconsumed, germination, or observed seedling establishment. Align Li and Lang autumn→spring for description, regardless of calendar order; neither is fully verified repeated within-year paired design. Keep annual Cao and Sachser separate. Never equate position with known movement history. Use joint outcome sets with A+B≤total possible success, not independent maxima of missing states. All missing allocations equally permitted, no sampling probability or CI. Each contrast is the difference of two source-specific convex outer sets; enumerate vertex pairs, verify prior total bounds. Cao deliberately allows broad post-removal ceiling and conservative rounding; this is not a complete fate partition.

**Distinguish actual compensation from compensation impossibility.** A recorded increase in one route offsets a recorded decrease in the other if opposite signs and sum≥0. Full compensation of a source-route loss is impossible if the entire source change interval is negative AND the entire joint total change interval is negative. This latter result does not show there was an actual gain in the moved route. Reverse orientation changes interpretation of compensation; do not compare V21 spring→autumn to V22 autumn→spring as opposite biological cases. Two cases agree after orientation alignment.

## What is missing for an empirical mechanism or prediction?
Zero rows meet a strict formal synthesis criterion (independent repeated landscapes, matching endpoint/horizon, complete source route/effort verification and sampling uncertainty). Four seasonal rows from just two landscapes; three Lang species share one capture/resource context, Li pools four species. Annual rows are different endpoints and cannot be pooled as seasonal replicates. Missing-fate bounds do not quantify sampling variation. No independent resource-pressure variable in both seasonal sources, no actor-specific causal role or prediction fit.

Proposed next test, not a result: freeze a prospective cohort-specific *early irreversible loss* predictor (confirmed predation by a predeclared early day, plus known baseline viable cohort fraction), and independently measure final source and moved emergence on the same experimental cohorts. If early irreversible loss already exceeds the previous period's failure fraction, full recovery to the previous period's total is mathematically impossible. The identity is not ecological novelty; empirical question is whether early animal-caused loss actually predicts terminal route compensation across held-out seasons and landscapes, after resource amount/composition, seed traits and independent consumer composition. Use matched duration and known missing fate, distinguish true nonmovement from final position, quantify effect uncertainty by landscape/year blocks, include failures/ties.
