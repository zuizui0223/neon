## Biological reason to test
In a retrospective Finnish bank vole cohort of 47 animals at ten forest sites, 102 within-site animal pairs showed identity-specific summer/fall captured-centroid distance-rank persistence above an autumn-ID-shuffle reference (site mean rho excess +0.469, descriptive CI +0.108 to +0.819). Does this say anything about ACTUAL animal proximity? No independent contact detector existed in that dataset, and identifying which vole pairs are relatively close is not the same as observing the same social partners touching or co-nesting.

A relevant 2025 study with DIFFERENT SPECIES and SOURCE explicitly compares two measurement channels in the same prairie vole population: Davidson, Solomon, Keane & Dantzer, Journal of Mammalogy 2025 DOI 10.1093/jmammal/gyaf032, "Methods for measuring fine-scale social proximity in small mammals". The single original seminatural 0.1 ha enclosed prairie vole population yielded 24 published matched animal nodes with both co-trapping and ProxLog (~5–6 cm BLE) detection networks. **Original authors already published MRQAP R²=0.02**, QAP r=0.148, and p-values, with low but positive correspondence. This is important external evidence AGAINST claiming that relative trap-derived proximity reliably predicts high-resolution actual observed dyad encounters, but **not** evidence that the original Finland pair rankings are wrong. Different species, time, enclosure, detector technology, and network estimands.
