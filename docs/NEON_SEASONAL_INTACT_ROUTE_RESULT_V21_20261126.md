## Parallel pathways
A=IS, B=IR+HR, M=MR (missing after removal), R=PR+IR+HR+MR. The operational endpoint T=A+B+mM (m in [0,1] for each season) counts intact/unconsumed-state seed fraction, NOT germination or proven viability. Keep reported categories fixed, let every missing seed be intact or not, don't assign it to source route. This is a stricter state contrast than inferring all moved seeds beneficial. Exact affine bounds conditional on rounded rates; no pseudocounts, no sampling CIs.

Even if every spring missing seed were intact and no autumn missing seed were intact, total intact-state fraction increases 5.11 pp. Source route increases 10.94 pp; moved route change ranges −5.83 to +45.95 pp. Thus source gain can cover even worst possible moved-route loss (1.8765 times that loss). This is partial identification of an endpoint-state balance, not evidence that movement actually declined or a causal compensatory mechanism. Confirmed moved intact increases 12.34 pp. Under unrestricted unknown fate assignments the total difference is +5.11 to +56.89 pp. Prior predation bound V1 already implied total direction; new contribution is route-specific guarantee, not an independent replication.
 
## Conditional pathway composition
p=A/(1−R), q=(B+mM)/R. Spring p=25.48%, q=28.27–69.31%; autumn p=74.80%, q=37.45–88.07%. Spring moved-minus-nonmoved conditional state difference positive across all assignments; autumn direction unresolved, equality requires ~73.8% of missing autumn seeds intact. In particular an apparent conditional movement advantage need not persist across seasons. No causal movement effect: seeds choosing/experiencing routes differ. No assumption of equal unknown fate fractions; that alternative is explicitly a sensitivity analysis. Record symmetric decomposition ΔT=ΔR(q̄−p̄)+(1−R̄)Δp+R̄Δq, with all four corner combinations and common-m sensitivity, verified algebraically. Unknown fate can change allocation and conditional removed terms, so do not claim their signs universally.
 
## Literature and source eligibility
Edwards & Crawley 1999 Oecologia DOI 10.1007/s004420050729 already show spring/autumn and sowing-density dependence of rodent effects on emergence, and later density-dependent compensation in two of four plants. That is prior art on seasonal recruitment compensation, not proof of dispersed versus source-route compensation. Lai et al. 2025 DOI 10.1111/1749-4877.12912 abstract reports winter/summer predation and hoarding as well as rapid summer germination, but no complete source-vs-moved germination counts; do not include in route test. Ma et al. 2010 Zhengzhou University journal 42:102–107, full institutional PDF (https://www5.zzu.edu.cn/__local/B/B5/12/585CD5D6338F3A461F6ED0C79BB_AFA9DF00_47BB1.pdf), 600 apricot seeds per release on 17 Apr/20 Jul/1 Oct 2008, 60 days, same Yugong Forest Farm, with a potentially overlapping 1800-seed publication PMID 20707107. PDF screenshot attempts returned cache miss, Table 3 text not reliable (zero single-seed spring caches inconsistent with abstract), no exact complementary route counts or seedling endpoint. Treat one source cluster and do not add a numerical independent replicate. No retrieval bypass. Prior V20 uses different years, no matched recruitment horizons, and true total establishment direction unresolved. The V21 comparison concerns intact-state only. Therefore seasonal establishment-route compensation remains unverified, not established by combining these two studies.
 
## Decision
Retain the central ecological question of whether resource/consumer conditions change the *relative reliance on source retention vs animal-mediated paths*, but require directly tracked comparable terminal recruitment in repeated seasonal cohorts. General seasonal compensation is known; novelty needs independent stage-specific route contrast with full fate partition and actual resource/consumer measurements. No formal meta-analysis, no new causal conclusion, no threshold relaxation or merge/submission.
