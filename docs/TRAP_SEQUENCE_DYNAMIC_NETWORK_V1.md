# Dynamic trap-sequence interaction network — result boundary v1

Date: 2026-10-07

Status: post-result exploratory synthesis.

## Central result

The San Jacinto rodent guild contains **directional sequence structure at the exact detector scale that is invisible to symmetric whole-night niche-overlap summaries, but that structure is not a fixed hierarchy**.

Pooled across adjacent within-night checks, two different analyses recover the same best six-species ordering:

**SKR > DKR > PEMA > CHFA > LAPM > PEER**

- trap × bout date-shuffle O/E network: 14/15 pairwise directions;
- trap + grid-date-transition fixed-effect network: 15/15 pairwise directions;
- the fixed-effect ordering remains unchanged when exact same-individual recurrence is removed.

The empirical body-mass ordering is unusually concordant with the pooled network.

## Spatial replication of the pooled order

The pooled order is not driven by one trapping grid.

Under the stronger trap + grid-date-transition fixed-effect network, omitting each grid in turn gives maximum pairwise scores of **14–15/15**. The full-data order

**SKR > DKR > PEMA > CHFA > LAPM > PEER**

remains among the optimal orders after **7/8** grid omissions. The one exception is omission of grid 2, where the unique optimum becomes the empirical body-mass order:

**SKR > DKR > CHFA > PEMA > PEER > LAPM**.

An independent date-shuffle leave-one-grid-out audit is even more stable: the full-data order remains optimal after **8/8** grid omissions.

Thus the pooled directional structure is spatially replicated, even though its exact lower-rank ordering is not immutable.

## Temporal reconfiguration

The pooled ordering decomposes differently across the night:

- EARLY→MIDDLE: unique 14/15 optimum, **SKR > DKR > PEMA > LAPM > CHFA > PEER**;
- MIDDLE→LATE: maximum 12/15 with three alternative optima;
- only 9/15 dyad directions are conserved across intervals;
- body-mass alignment is 12/15 early→middle and 8/15 middle→late.

Therefore the pooled ordering is **not evidence for one static dominance hierarchy**.

## Spatial scale

The clearest kangaroo-rat/pocket-mouse asymmetry is detector-local. It is present at the same trap but not at adjacent traps 6.25–8.84 m away.

This confines interpretation to **trap-mediated interaction history or cue response**, not a broad spatial exclusion zone.

## Relation to coarse niche inference

The original spatial SIM9 result is reproduced exactly at 8/32 segregated grid-seasons and is unchanged after keeping only first identifiable captures within individual-nights.

Temporal Czekanowski/RA3 classifications are much more representation-sensitive, but coarse temporal segregation remains rare across ALL, FIRST, RANDOM and normalized representations.

Thus symmetric coarse-scale niche overlap and directed fine-scale sequence structure answer different ecological questions.

## Strongest ecological gap

> **When interaction effects are asymmetric and change over hours, can community-level niche overlap conceal the direction and temporal reorganization of species interactions?**

The present dataset supplies a strong exploratory example but cannot identify whether the detector-local sequence structure is caused by natural interference, residual scent, capture/release, bait/trap state, or combinations of these processes.

## Claim boundary

Allowed:
- time-varying directional interaction network;
- trap-mediated sequence structure;
- directional cue-response structure;
- pooled ordering concordant with body size.

Not allowed:
- fixed dominance hierarchy;
- causal interference competition;
- natural encounter network;
- scent-mediated hierarchy;
- competitive exclusion.

## Next discriminating evidence

The highest-value next evidence is external rather than more same-data fishing:

1. an independent repeated-check dataset;
2. an experiment manipulating previous-occupant cue / trap cleaning / release history;
3. direct behavioural observations that can distinguish natural avoidance from trap-mediated response.
