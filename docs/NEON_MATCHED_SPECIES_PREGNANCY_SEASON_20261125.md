## Distinct prospective exploratory direct mammal endpoint

McLean & Guralnick (2021), Ecology, PMID 33226631, Dryad 10.5061/dryad.zgmsbcc8w, already examine broad-scale deer mouse reproductive phenology and climate drivers. Stephens et al. (2024), JAE 10.1111/1365-2656.14148, already document different resource-timing responses of co-occurring species. Temporal partitioning is well-established; no novelty claim for the general idea. The narrowly defined question is whether **relative seasonal changes in recorded pregnancy prevalence of co-observed species, measured at the same plots and sampling windows, transfer between independent physical sites**. This is a separate observation endpoint, not a rescue of seedfall linkage or validation of exact-trap recurrence. No feeding, causal competition, demographic recruitment, true breeding onset, or coexistence claimed.

Same pre-existing 32-site product intersection and 2017–2025 RELEASE-2026 selected mammal manifest of 1,760 files, exact full manifest pinned; only mammal rows downloaded, all four whole-site shards. Fixed early May–June and late August–September calendar windows, no latitude or climate tuning; summer July and other months do not define response. Use existing completed three-night protocol event gate, >=90 canonical trap coordinates each night, first eligible bout in each window per plot/year chosen independently of captures and pregnancy. This is a seasonal contrast of actual sampled windows, not peak or onset dates. Captures use documented NEON status codes as in standardized ecocomDP (https://rdrr.io/github/EDIorg/ecocomDP/src/NEON_dev_script/old%20dev%20files/map_neon.ecocomdp.10072.001.001_20220712.R); target taxa from official taxonomy helper, exact species binomial/rank. Adult female, literal tags only; exclude observed taxon/sex conflicts and replacement-tag histories in downloaded months. No claim of fully resolved tag histories; first per tag within phase across shared plots, same-day conflicting status treated as unknown. Distinct site-year taxon pair requires both species' tagged adult females observed in both windows of each included plot; at least five known pregnant/not unique tags per species per window after pooling those same plots. This lower threshold is a separately defined new endpoint, not applied to the failed resource endpoint. Restricts inference to well-observed co-occurring species. Missing/unknown pregnancy not negative; keep unknown as worst-case marginal bounds.

D = (late minus early observed pregnancy rate of species A) minus (late minus early of B), alphabetically fixed taxon-ID order. Equal site-year mean within pair/site. Full eligible counts retained. Cross-site transfer evaluation requires at least two eligible years at each of at least three physical sites for a species pair. Predict each held-out site's D by mean from other sites for the same pair. Ties count as non-agreement; report equal-site sign agreement, MSE against fixed zero contrast baseline, unknown status sensitivity, all eligible repeated pairs. Pre-effect exploratory positive support requires >=8 held-out sites, >=3 repeated pairs, >=70% mean sign agreement, positive MSE gain over zero and >=75% sites majority correct; otherwise report thin support or nonsupport. No significance claim, no independent species-pair assumption, no threshold/window revision after result. Shared calendar windows do not equate temperature/food/phenological stage. Differential capture, cohort composition, adult age, geographic climate, reproductive-state duration and measurement error can influence results. This test cannot establish reproductive temporal niche partitioning as a coexistence mechanism. If sufficient replicated evidence, only then connect to spatial recurrence by a separate test.
