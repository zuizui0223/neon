# San Jacinto positional aliasing — final held-out result

Date: 2026-09-29

Decision: **authorize_live_trap_positional_aliasing_result**.

## Held-out confirmatory species

### Peromyscus maniculatus (PEMA)

- 485 repeat-capture individual-nights
- 352 nights (72.6%) shifted by >= one 6.25 m trap spacing
- 95% Wilson CI: 68.4–76.4%
- median first-to-last distance: 8.84 m
- 90th percentile: 25.0 m
- spatial replication: 7 eligible grids passed the >25% rule

### Peromyscus eremicus (PEER)

- 107 repeat-capture individual-nights
- 74 nights (69.2%) shifted by >= one 6.25 m trap spacing
- 95% Wilson CI: 59.9–77.1%
- median first-to-last distance: 6.25 m
- 90th percentile: 19.76 m
- spatial replication: all 3 eligible grids passed

## Why this is stronger than the discovery observation

The heteromyid first/last pattern motivated the question, but PEMA and PEER outcomes were not inspected before this effect lock. Species selection was based only on effect-blind repeat-capture support.

Both species independently exceeded the pre-specified materiality criterion by a wide margin, and the result replicated spatially across grids.

## Link to the general theory

The separate theory branch proves deterministic triangle-inequality bounds on how within-night positional spans can propagate into inter-night movement and population MPD estimates. The empirical result establishes that the within-night span entering those bounds is not merely theoretical or rare in this live-trapping design.

## Claim boundary

This is a positional-aliasing result. It does not claim that first or last capture is biologically correct, does not estimate home range, and does not imply universal bias across all small-mammal trapping designs.
