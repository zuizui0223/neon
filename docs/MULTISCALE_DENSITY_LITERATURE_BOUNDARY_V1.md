# Literature boundary — multiscale density accommodation v1

**Date:** 2026-10-05

## Already occupied territory

### Density and individual home range

Density-dependent contraction of individual home ranges is well established across mammals.

A particularly important direct competitor is:

O'Fallon S, Pinter-Wollman N, Mabry KE. 2025. *Uncovering multiple influences on space use by deer mice using large ecological networks*. Oecologia 207:98. doi:10.1007/s00442-025-05731-2.

Using 10 years of NEON data from 36 sites and 2,420 *Peromyscus* individuals, that study reported:

- males use larger home ranges than females;
- home-range area decreases as density increases;
- habitat, latitude and density interact to influence space use.

Therefore those statements are not available as the novelty of the current programme.

### Cross-scale and within/between-individual space use

The distinction between within-individual and between-individual spatial variation is also established in movement and individual-specialization ecology. Spatial niche studies have explicitly partitioned individual and population space use, and SCR literature jointly treats individual movement scale and population density.

Therefore the variance decomposition itself is not novel.

## Remaining gap

The candidate gap is narrower:

> **When abundance changes within standardized small-mammal sampling sessions, how is that density change distributed between individual-scale space use and the population-level spatial footprint?**

The key object is the *difference between scale responses*, not either response alone.

## Why the gap matters

If individual home ranges contract with density but the population footprint expands, crowding is accommodated at both scales.

If individual home ranges contract while the population footprint remains stable, additional individuals are accommodated primarily by tighter packing.

If both scales contract, density increase would coincide with population-level spatial compression.

These states can have different consequences for:

- encounter rates;
- competition;
- transmission;
- local resource depletion;
- interpretation of trap-based abundance and space-use monitoring.

## Candidate novelty sentence

> **We test whether density dependence in small mammals is conserved across spatial scales or redistributed between individual space use and the population footprint.**

## Claims to avoid

Do not claim novelty for:

- density-dependent home-range contraction;
- habitat effects on deer-mouse home range;
- NEON as a large-network behavioural ecology resource;
- within-versus-between-individual variance decomposition itself.

Do not describe trap-based coordinates as complete movement paths.

## Position relative to the current live-trap aliasing paper

PR #21 asks whether temporal representation of repeated within-night locations changes a downstream estimator.

This programme asks a biological question about **how populations accommodate density spatially**.

The two papers may share data infrastructure, but neither is evidence for the other's biological conclusion.
