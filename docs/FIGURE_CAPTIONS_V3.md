## Figure 1. Temporal positional aliasing and deterministic sensitivity bounds

A single ecological occasion can contain more than one valid observed spatial state for the same marked individual. For occasions $t$ and $u$, $F$ and $L$ denote first and last observed positions, and $\delta$ is the within-occasion first-to-last positional span. The difference between FIRST→FIRST and LAST→LAST inter-occasion movement estimates is bounded by $\delta_t+\delta_u$. For a population represented by paired first and last positions, the corresponding mean-pairwise-distance difference is bounded by twice the mean within-occasion span. These inequalities are sensitivity bounds, not expected biases and not claims that FIRST or LAST is biologically correct.

## Figure 2. Temporal representation changes SCR spatial scale only when the within-night observation process changes state

**A.** Stationary negative controls. Median relative sigma bias for CHECK, FIRST and LAST encodings across generating sigma values of 6.25, 12.5 and 25 m. All median biases remain within 4% of the generating value; dashed lines denote ±10%.  
**B.** Empirically anchored state-transition simulations. Paired LAST/FIRST sigma ratios are shown for POST transitions, in which the baseline state precedes the injected empirical detector transition, and mirrored PRE transitions, in which the ordering is reversed. The solid line at one denotes rule invariance; dashed lines at 0.9 and 1.1 denote the pre-specified 10% practical sensitivity band. Reversing the state order reverses the direction of FIRST/LAST sensitivity. Each cell used 40 Monte Carlo replicates. CHECK-level fits retained all detections but did not consistently recover the baseline generating sigma once checks sampled multiple spatial states.

## Figure 3. Prospectively held-out validation of positional aliasing

Fraction of repeat-capture individual-nights in which first and last observed locations differed by at least one 6.25-m trap spacing for two prospectively held-out Cricetidae species. Large points show species-level fractions with two-sided 95% Wilson intervals; smaller points show eligible trapping-grid fractions. The vertical dashed line is the frozen 25% materiality threshold. *Peromyscus maniculatus* showed material shifts on 72.6% of 485 repeat-capture nights (95% CI 68.4–76.4%; 7/7 eligible grids passed), and *P. eremicus* on 69.2% of 107 nights (59.9–77.1%; 3/3 grids passed).

## Figure 4. Denominator context and magnitude of observed within-night positional spans

**A.** Fraction of all valid individual-nights that were repeat-observed, and fraction of all valid individual-nights on which a ≥1-trap-spacing first-to-last shift was directly observed. Because single-capture nights cannot reveal first-to-last change, the latter values are observational lower bounds on the realized all-night material-shift fraction, not estimates of latent movement or the generating probability.  
**B.** First-to-last span magnitude among repeat-capture individual-nights, expressed in units of the 6.25-m trap spacing. Circles indicate medians and triangles indicate 90th percentiles; vertical segments connect the two. The dashed horizontal line denotes one trap spacing. Median spans were 1.41 trap spacings for *P. maniculatus* and 1.00 for *P. eremicus*; 90th percentiles were 4.00 and 3.16 trap spacings, respectively.
