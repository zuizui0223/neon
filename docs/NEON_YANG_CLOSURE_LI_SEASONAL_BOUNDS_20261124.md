## Li et al. 2021: seasonal predation is identifiable under missing-fate extremes; higher autumn caching is not

Original open-access publication DOI [10.3897/zookeys.1025.60972](https://doi.org/10.3897/zookeys.1025.60972), [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7997858/), Results “Seed fate” and Fig. 4, Methods release design and six categories. Separate temperate forest in Northeast China, four seed species, spring 15 stations / 1,200 releases, autumn 9 stations / 720 releases. The paper reports pooled fate percentages; these are manually transcribed as printed, not raw individual records. Do not infer exact count denominators from rounded rates (several spring percentages are not consistent with simple rounding of integer counts out of 1,200). No assumption that every seed was an independent replicate or that the station rosters were identical across seasons. Source reproduction is CC BY 4.0 with attribution; only numeric facts and paraphrased variable definitions retained.

For each season, known predation = PS + PR, known cache = HR, known intact moved = IR + HR, and unknown = MR.
