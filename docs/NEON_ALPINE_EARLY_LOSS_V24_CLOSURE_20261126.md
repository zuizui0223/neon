## Novelty gate
Bogdziewicz, Crone & Zwolak (2020) DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.13307 already modeled net interaction outcomes from several vital rates and found burial benefits insufficient to offset predation. Joyce et al. (2024) DOI 10.1111/btp.13381 explicitly measured later seedling establishment after vertebrate predation. Ortega et al. (2021) dataset DOI 10.5061/dryad.tqjq2bvvk already supports a global meta-analysis of separate seed removal and recruitment endpoints. Wrobel et al. (2025) already jointly measured seed crops, captured rodents, tracked seed fate, and seedling emergence across three years. Do not claim generic early-loss constraint, predator satiation, or stage non-proportionality is newly discovered.

The remaining original question is whether a *separately measured* early predation indicator improves prediction of later open-minus-excluded emergence, beyond resource supply, consumer pressure and baseline recruitment, under holdout by landscape and time. Distinguish that prediction from the arithmetic upper bound of eventual survival among survivors of the same cohort. A suitable next data candidate is Joyce et al. 2024, Dryad 10.5061/dryad.05qfttfb9, whose public README lists seed_removal.csv, seed_tagging.csv, seedling_establishment.csv, predator detections and litter depth from 2021–23, with species/site/forest/station labels in the emergence file. This is metadata evidence only: no original file access or matched cohort validation in V24. A new analysis would require verifying actual matching experiment and calendar keys, not inferring shared seed identities from station codes. The separate excluded/access comparison and movement routes are distinct estimands; no direct seasonal inference without repeated comparable seasons. Do not return to capture-only prediction.
