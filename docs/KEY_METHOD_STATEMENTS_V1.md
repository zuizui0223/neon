# Key methodological statements — live-trap aliasing v1

## 30-second editor summary

Ecologists often define occasions more coarsely than observations are collected. If the same marked individual is observed at multiple locations within one occasion, collapsing those observations to one location creates an unreported spatial-state choice. We introduce a lightweight diagnostic that measures within-occasion positional span relative to a user-defined material scale and translates that span into deterministic sensitivity bounds for downstream spatial statistics.

Simulation shows when repeat-observation-conditioned summaries are representative and when informative repeat observation can bias them. A prospectively held-out two-species validation shows that material within-night positional shifts are frequent and spatially replicated under a repeated-check live-trapping protocol.

## Central claim

> Temporal positional aliasing can be diagnosed before spatial observations are collapsed to one state, allowing analysts to identify when within-occasion positional uncertainty is material at the scale of the planned analysis and to bound its potential effect on selected downstream spatial statistics.

## Strongest empirical claim

> In two prospectively held-out Cricetidae species, at least one trap spacing of first-to-last positional change occurred on roughly 70% of repeat-capture individual-nights and replicated across all eligible trapping grids.

## Safest all-night claim

> Even when all valid individual-nights are used as the denominator and single-capture nights are treated as unresolved, material first-to-last shifts were directly observed on at least 28.9% of *Peromyscus maniculatus* nights and 24.6% of *P. eremicus* nights.

## What this paper does not claim

- It does not estimate an unrestricted movement path.
- It does not identify FIRST or LAST as the correct biological location.
- It does not estimate home-range bias.
- It does not claim temporal aggregation is a newly discovered problem.
- It does not assume repeat-observed nights are representative of all nights.
- It does not generalize the empirical magnitude to all live-trapping protocols or taxa.
