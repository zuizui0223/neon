# Trap-mediated interaction history in the San Jacinto rodent guild — discovery v1

Date: 2026-10-07

Status: **post-result exploratory ecological discovery**.

## Question

Can a rodent guild show strong fine-scale, directional interaction structure within a night even when coarse temporal niche-overlap analysis shows no temporal segregation?

## Discovery result

Using early -> middle and middle -> late same-detector transitions, with a date-shuffle reference inside grid × detector × trapping bout × check pair:

- every focal species shows excess same-species succession at the same detector;
- pooled same-individual recurrence is strong, but excess different-known-individual conspecific succession remains for DKR, PEMA and SKR;
- kangaroo rat (DKR or SKR) -> LAPM transitions are deficient, while LAPM -> kangaroo rat transitions are near null;
- the KR -> LAPM deficit survives every leave-one-grid-out analysis and a grid × trapping-bout cluster bootstrap;
- the deficit is strongest at the exact previous detector, weaker at adjacent detectors, and absent by Manhattan distance 2;
- across the pooled six-species guild, the directed transition network admits a near-transitive ordering that broadly aligns with body mass, but interval-specific analyses show that this ordering reconfigures within the night.

## Ecological interpretation

The strongest current interpretation is **microtemporal trap-mediated interaction structure**.

The pattern is compatible with interference-competition theory because:
- kangaroo rats are the largest species in this guild;
- previous enclosure experiments showed body-size dominance and LAPM avoidance of DKR;
- the field niche-partitioning study itself predicted the largest kangaroo rats should most influence segregation under interference competition.

But the field trapping design does not identify mechanism. Animals were captured, confined, handled and released at the detector, so same-trap transitions can contain residual odour, trap response, bait state, local activity and natural avoidance.

The spatial decay is therefore important: a strong distance-0 effect with little persistence at distance 1–2 is not evidence for a broad natural exclusion zone. It instead points to an extremely local history signal.

## Candidate general principle

> Marginal temporal niche overlap can miss **time-varying directional interaction structure** in event sequences.

This general principle is not new in animal ecology; fine-scale time-to-encounter studies in carnivores already show avoidance despite broad diel overlap. The potentially useful contribution here is different: repeated live trapping can reveal—and itself mediate—microtemporal directional structure in a small-mammal community. The pooled network resembles independently established body-size dominance, but its interval-specific reconfiguration shows that it should not be treated as a fixed hierarchy.

## Claim boundary

This branch must not say:
- trapping proves natural competition;
- residual scent is the identified mechanism;
- KR causes LAPM exclusion in the wild;
- the exploratory San Jacinto transition effect is prospectively confirmed.
