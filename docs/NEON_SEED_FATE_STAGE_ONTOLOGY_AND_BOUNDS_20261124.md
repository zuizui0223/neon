## Frozen stage-bound estimator (implemented with synthetic tests, not applied to original source yet)

Original paper methods: 1,620 individually tagged seeds, three sites, three feeding stations per site, at each station 60 mono chestnuts + 60 mono acorns + mixed 30 chestnuts/30 acorns. Late October–early December 2008, one-week follow-up, no multi-season comparison. Acorn-specific paired original 9-station contrast, equal station within site and equal site weights. Require all 90 released acorns at every station (60 mono and 30 mixed) reconciled into mutually exclusive intact cache, embryo-damaged cache, confirmed noncache, unresolved fate, or confirmed cache with unknown embryo condition. No seed identity deduplication, date or category guessing; no output if source schema cannot establish those denominators. Known noncache must have an explicit outcome (remaining/eaten etc.); missing retrieval remains unresolved. Compare cumulative cache and intact-cache probabilities per ALL released seeds. Define attenuation A = delta P(cache) − delta P(intact cache) = delta P(damaged cache) at complete-data level. Compute sharp marginal worst-case bounds for each stage by letting unresolved fate/embryo status vary; bound attenuation directly from damaged-cache categories rather than subtracting two independent marginal bounds. Directional descriptive criterion: lower bounds for both cumulative cache gain and attenuation >0 over the equal-site summary, and observed known-damage difference positive at each of the three sites. No p-value claim, no independence of 1,620 seeds, no evidence of germination, establishment or seasonal switching. If the bound includes zero, no robust stage-attenuation claim. No same-data category changes after effect extraction.

The published models already document caching increase and lack of detectable intact dispersal increase in acorns. The original authors themselves proposed stage-based indirect interactions; our additional question is robustness of the stage difference to unresolved fates, not novelty of this biological concept.
