# Temporal exchangeability and representation stability

Date: 2026-10-03

Status: exact conceptual result for the live-trap positional-aliasing paper.

## 1. Why raw positional aliasing is not itself a bias estimate

Let one ecological occasion contain an ordered sequence of detector outcomes

\[
Y_1,\ldots,Y_K,
\]

where \(Y_j=0\) denotes no detection and \(Y_j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\) denotes the detector at which an individual is observed on check \(j\).

Define FIRST as the detector on the earliest non-zero check and LAST as the detector on the latest non-zero check.

Large distances between FIRST and LAST show that a one-location representation is not unique. They do **not**, by themselves, imply that FIRST and LAST target different population-level spatial parameters.

## 2. Exchangeability null

Assume that, conditional on the latent spatial state relevant to the fitted model, the check-level detector outcomes are temporally exchangeable.

A sufficient special case is that all checks are generated independently from the same detector distribution around one fixed activity centre, with the same detector availability and detection parameters on each check.

Under time reversal,

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_K) \overset{d}= (Y_K,\ldots,Y_1).
\]

Time reversal exchanges FIRST and LAST while leaving the event “at least one detection” unchanged.

Therefore

\[
\mathrm{FIRST} \overset{d}= \mathrm{LAST}.
\]

The same statement applies jointly across independent individuals and occasions. Consequently, any systematic population-level difference between FIRST- and LAST-based estimators cannot be generated by the temporal label alone under this exchangeability null.

This result does **not** say that FIRST and LAST are equal in a realized finite dataset. They can differ substantially within individual occasions, and finite-sample fitted parameters can differ. It says that neither rule has a directional population-level advantage under an exchangeable static observation process.

## 3. Time-reversal non-identifiability of span-only diagnostics

For an occasion with first and last detector positions \(F_t,L_t\), define the span

\[
\delta_t=d(F_t,L_t).
\]

Reverse the within-occasion time order. Then

\[
F_t' = L_t,\qquad L_t' = F_t,
\]

but

\[
\delta_t'=d(F_t',L_t')=d(L_t,F_t)=\delta_t.
\]

Thus all diagnostics depending only on unordered spans are invariant to time reversal, including:

- the fraction with \(\delta_t\ge s\);
- median and quantiles of \(\delta_t\);
- directly observed all-occasion lower bounds based on the same materiality event;
- any summary of the empirical distribution of \(\delta_t\).

By contrast, a directed downstream contrast exchanges sign under reversal. For a scalar estimator \(T\),

\[
C = T(\mathrm{LAST})-T(\mathrm{FIRST})
\]

becomes

\[
C'=-C.
\]

For a positive estimator, the log-ratio

\[
R=\log\{T(\mathrm{LAST})/T(\mathrm{FIRST})\}
\]

becomes \(-R\).

Therefore the sign of a FIRST-versus-LAST downstream effect is not identifiable from span-only aliasing summaries.

This is an exact reason that the positional-aliasing screen and the downstream stability check must be separate stages.

## 4. What breaks exchangeability

FIRST and LAST can become distributionally different when the observation process has temporal ordering, for example:

- capture or handling changes the subsequent spatial state;
- detector availability or effort changes through the occasion;
- attraction, avoidance or trap response changes after detection;
- natural movement creates a directional or non-stationary within-occasion state process;
- check timing samples different behavioural states;
- weather or other time-varying conditions alter the detection kernel.

The relevant issue is not movement per se. An animal may move substantially while remaining approximately exchangeable around the same fitted spatial kernel. The issue is whether temporal order changes the distribution sampled by later observations.

## 5. Connection to the San Jacinto results

The held-out empirical screen found frequent positional non-uniqueness:

- PEMA: 72.6% of repeat-capture nights shifted by at least one trap spacing;
- PEER: 69.2%.

Yet the post-stop exploratory PEMA SCR comparison found:

- sigma_FIRST = 8.8515 m;
- sigma_LAST = 8.5625 m;
- LAST/FIRST = 0.9673;
- relative change = -3.27%.

Thus large raw positional aliasing did not automatically produce a material directional sigma effect.

The stationary SCR simulations give the corresponding mechanistic null: when repeated checks sample one static spatial state, FIRST and LAST have no systematic ordering advantage.

The ordered state-transition simulations deliberately break exchangeability. Mirroring the temporal ordering preserves the same span distribution but reverses the FIRST/LAST sigma effect. This is the empirical-computational demonstration of the time-reversal argument above.

## 6. Methodological workflow implied by the result

The method should be presented as two linked diagnostics.

### Stage A — positional non-uniqueness screen

Ask whether multiple observed states within one nominal occasion differ materially relative to a prechosen spatial scale.

This stage answers:

> Is temporal aggregation hiding spatial information that could matter?

It does not estimate downstream bias.

### Stage B — representation-stability test

Re-fit the intended downstream analysis under defensible temporal representations, or use a model-specific generative benchmark when the empirical analysis is not estimable.

This stage answers:

> Does the downstream estimand materially change when the temporal representation changes?

Only Stage B can establish estimand instability for a specific analysis.

## 7. Consequence for claims

The paper should not claim that positional aliasing generally biases SCR sigma.

A stronger and more accurate claim is:

> Frequent within-occasion positional aliasing is a warning condition, not a bias estimate. Under a temporally exchangeable static observation process, FIRST and LAST are distributionally equivalent even when their realized locations often differ. Systematic downstream divergence requires temporal structure that breaks exchangeability; therefore raw aliasing and model-specific estimand stability must be diagnosed separately.

This distinction is general to repeated-location ecological data and is not specific to live trapping or SCR.
