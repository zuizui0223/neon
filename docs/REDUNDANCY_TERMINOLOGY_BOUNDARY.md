# Redundancy terminology boundary

The paper uses **redundancy** in a deliberately narrow, property-specific sense.

A species is an *individually sufficient continuity carrier* at a site when its observed occurrences alone satisfy every prespecified trap-neighbourhood adjacency criterion used in that site.

When several species independently satisfy that same declared property, the property is called **species-redundant**.

This does **not** mean that the species are ecologically interchangeable, functionally equivalent, or redundant for ecosystem functioning in general.

That distinction is important because the broader functional-redundancy literature warns against treating species as generally exchangeable. Eisenhauer et al. (2023) specifically argue that redundancy language should always identify the function and environmental context under consideration.

Accordingly, this paper always attaches redundancy to one explicit property:

> observed-occurrence spatial continuity under the declared trap-neighbourhood criteria.

The among-site carrier-turnover result therefore means:

> different taxa can independently satisfy the same measured spatial-continuity property in different sites.

It does not mean that they occupy the same niche or perform the same ecosystem function.

References:
- Laliberté et al. 2010. Ecology Letters 13:76–86. DOI 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01403.x.
- Eisenhauer et al. 2023. npj Biodiversity 2:9. DOI 10.1038/s44185-023-00015-5.
