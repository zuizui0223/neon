# Significance Statement — Oikos submission form

Ecological aggregation is often interpreted through complementarity: different species compensate for one another and generate a community-level property that no species provides alone. Using a response-naive, prospectively frozen analysis of 16 NEON small-mammal sites, we found the opposite organization for observed spatial continuity. Pooling species never increased continuity beyond the best individual species, yet the species independently carrying that continuity changed strongly among sites. The result reveals a two-scale structure — **within-site redundancy with among-site turnover in carrier identity** — and distinguishes turnover in *who provides* an aggregate property from turnover that is *required to create* the property. This distinction is relevant beyond small mammals because many community-level patterns may be inherited redundantly from component species rather than produced by complementarity.
