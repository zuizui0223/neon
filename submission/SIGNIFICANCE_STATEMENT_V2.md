# Significance Statement — Oikos submission form, V2

Ecological aggregation is often interpreted through complementarity: different species compensate for one another and generate a community-level property that no species provides alone. This study tests whether that logic extends from the insurance and metacommunity literature to a different aggregate property — spatial continuity of observed occurrences. In a response-naive, prospectively frozen analysis of 16 National Ecological Observatory Network small-mammal sites, pooling species never increased continuity beyond the best individual species, yet the species independently carrying continuity changed strongly among sites. The result reveals **within-site redundancy with among-site turnover in carrier identity**, distinguishing turnover in *who provides* an aggregate property from turnover that is *required to create* it.

The manuscript does not cite prior work by the author and is not an extension of an author-developed empirical result to a new taxon or region. Instead, it builds on published metacommunity, spatial-insurance and redundancy concepts by testing their complementarity logic against a deliberately different aggregate spatial property. This makes Oikos an appropriate outlet because the contribution is a general distinction among complementarity, property-specific redundancy and carrier turnover, rather than a taxon-specific small-mammal result.
