# Significance Statement — Oikos submission form, V3

Ecological turnover is often interpreted through complementarity: different species combine to generate or stabilize a community-level property. We show that a different organization can dominate. In 16 prospectively frozen small-mammal sites, pooling species never produced local spatial cohesion beyond the best individual species, yet the species carrying that property changed strongly among sites and spanned granivorous, herbivorous, omnivorous and predatory roles. Thus different ecosystem-effect roles can converge on the same spatial response property without being functionally interchangeable.

An independent response-naive 11-site mechanism validation then showed that carrier status is not a fixed species syndrome. After conditioning on each species' number of positive traps, observed spatial organization did not systematically increase carrier probability. Preserving positive-trap counts within mammal grids reproduced carrier status almost exactly, and 10 of 15 repeatedly observed species switched between carrier and non-carrier states among sites. The study therefore identifies local spatial cohesion as a context-dependent species × site state, separating turnover in *who carries* a property from complementarity that is *required to create* it.

The manuscript does not cite prior author-authored work and is not an extension of an author-developed empirical result. It tests a general ecological distinction among complementarity, property-specific redundancy, response-state convergence and taxonomic replaceability.
